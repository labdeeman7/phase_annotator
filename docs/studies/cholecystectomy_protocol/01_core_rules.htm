Annotate the dominant visible surgical objective over time—not a single instrument, isolated motion, or presumed off-screen intention.

Rules that apply throughout

  1. Cover the complete available recording. A partial recording does not need to begin at P1 or end at P7.
  2. Place a boundary when the new operative objective becomes clear and sustained.
  3. Do not force the nominal phase order. Phases may repeat, be absent, or occur in an unusual order.
  4. Record a genuine return to an earlier task. Sequences such as P1 → P2 → P1 → P2 and repeated P4 or P6 episodes are valid in complex cases.
  5. Brief supporting actions remain in the surrounding phase. Change only when that action becomes the dominant task.
  6. Do not infer an invisible action or future task.
  7. Use notes for meaningful ambiguity, unusual workflow, or prolonged inactivity.
  8. Use Undefined when the visible activity genuinely cannot be classified, plus the documented interim gallbladder-drainage exception.

Complex operations may be non-linear

Do not smooth a complex operation into one pass through P1–P7. If focused Calot dissection stops and renewed adhesiolysis or general exposure becomes dominant, return to P1; change back to P2 if focused Calot dissection later resumes. Retain every genuine recurrence.

Boundary test

Before

What is the surgeon mainly trying to achieve immediately before this moment?

After

What has become the sustained objective immediately after this moment?

Evidence

Which anatomy, action sequence and instrument use demonstrate that change?
