# Rough Cholec80 Review Notes — 4 October 2026

Status: **Archived working notes. Not a doctor-facing protocol.**

These notes preserve the reviewer's original case-by-case reasoning, uncertainty, reactions, and candidate rules from the ten-case Cholec80 walkthrough. They are retained because the polished protocol necessarily compresses the evidence and may later need to be challenged or revised.

The preserved reference manual derived from these notes is [`../CHOLEC80_DERIVED_TRAINING_MANUAL.md`](../CHOLEC80_DERIVED_TRAINING_MANUAL.md). The later [`../OAUTHC_CHOLECYSTECTOMY_TRAINING_MANUAL.md`](../OAUTHC_CHOLECYSTECTOMY_TRAINING_MANUAL.md) records the explicit OAUTHC adaptation. This archive explains how the original Cholec80-derived decisions arose.

Minor spelling and punctuation have been repaired only where needed for readability. The substance, doubts, outliers, and preferred examples have been preserved.

## General observations

- Cholec80 does not appear to use perfectly reproducible, frame-exact visual boundaries. Similar transitions can differ by several seconds.
- Some supplied boundaries occur shortly before a new instrument becomes visible, some when it appears, and some after it begins working.
- A consistent local protocol is still needed. The preferred approach is to use a visible, defensible cue rather than reproduce apparent timing noise.
- Expected phase order is not compulsory. Phases can be absent or appear in a different order.
- `video19` begins in Calot triangle dissection and has no Preparation phase.
- `video50` has no Cleaning/coagulation phase.
- `video79`, `video64`, and `video37` place Cleaning/coagulation before Packaging.
- `video14` places Retraction before a final Cleaning/coagulation phase and ends in Cleaning/coagulation.

## P1 — Preparation

### Initial working idea

Preparation covers the opening setup until focused Calot triangle dissection begins. If the recording begins part-way through surgery, Preparation should not be invented. `video19` begins directly in Calot triangle dissection.

The recurring cue is appearance of the hook or other dissection instrument. If it appears and begins working shortly afterwards, use its appearance as the transition. If it appears but remains idle or leaves the view, wait until it returns or starts purposeful movement.

### Case observations

- `video01`: the instrument appears roughly seven seconds before it starts working; the supplied change occurs about two seconds after it appears.
- `video04`: the hook appears and works almost immediately; the supplied transition occurs as it appears.
- `video14`: the hook appears and the transition is almost immediate.
- `video19`: Preparation is absent because the recording starts during Calot triangle dissection.
- `video37`: the transition occurs when the tip of the hook appears. A boundary approximately one second later would also have seemed defensible.
- `video44`: the supplied transition occurs approximately one second before the hook appears. This is visually difficult to justify.
- `video50`: the transition occurs approximately 0.3 seconds after the hook appears and is a strong example.
- `video64`: the hook appears, the camera is adjusted, and the transition follows. This suggests allowing a short beat rather than requiring immediate work.
- `video68`: the transition occurs while the hook is still being advanced, around three seconds before the reviewer's preferred cue. This may indicate annotation variability or information beyond the visible image.
- `video79`: the hook appears, leaves the field, returns, and only then is the phase changed; it waits several more seconds before working. This is the main counterexample to a simple “instrument first appears” rule.

### Candidate rule from the review

Use the appearance of the dissection instrument when it remains present and works shortly afterwards. If it appears but goes idle or leaves, use its purposeful return or the beginning of work.

Preferred teaching examples noted during review: `video04`, `video14`, and `video50`; `video01` can demonstrate a slightly delayed start. `video79` is the caveat.

## P2 — Calot triangle dissection

### Initial working idea

P2 begins at the end of Preparation, or at video start when the recording already shows Calot triangle dissection. It ends when the clipper or an equivalent instrument intended to occlude the duct or artery appears for the clipping task.

The instrument may differ in project videos, so the intended clipping/ligation action matters more than one exact device.

### Case observations

- `video01`: the transition is associated with the clipper appearing rather than the hook leaving.
- `video04`: the transition follows clipper appearance by roughly one beat.
- `video14`: the transition is immediate when the clipper appears.
- `video19`: the supplied phase changes approximately three seconds before the clipper is visible.
- `video37`: both graspers remain steady while the phase changes; the clipper appears approximately three seconds later. No visible image cue explains the early boundary.
- `video44`: the clipper appears approximately two seconds after the supplied transition.
- `video50`: the phase changes roughly half a second after the clipper appears.
- `video64`: clipper appearance and the phase change are nearly simultaneous.
- `video68`: the phase changes immediately when the clipper appears.
- `video79`: the phase changes around half a beat after clipper appearance.

### Candidate rule from the review

Use purposeful clipper/ligation-instrument appearance. Do not ask clinicians to imitate unexplained boundaries two or three seconds before anything changes visually.

Preferred examples noted during review: `video01`, `video50`, and `video64`.

## P3 — Clipping and cutting

### Initial working idea

P3 includes clipping or tying the cystic duct and artery and cutting the secured structures. The end is difficult because the supplied boundary often falls in the interval between the final cut and the beginning of gallbladder dissection.

The reviewer considered using the final cut, scissors leaving, a brief observation period, or appearance of the next hook. The emerging preference was: wait briefly to confirm that no more clipping/cutting remains, then change before purposeful gallbladder dissection begins.

### Case observations

- `video01`: approximately 15 seconds separate scissors leaving from hook arrival; the supplied transition occurs roughly halfway between them.
- `video04`: the phase changes immediately after the scissors make the final cut and exit.
- `video14`: the phase changes when the hook appears—the opposite end of the ambiguous interval.
- `video19`: another boundary roughly halfway between final cutting and hook appearance.
- `video37`: the transition occurs about two seconds after cutting/scissors departure and about four seconds before hook arrival.
- `video44`: another intermediate boundary without a single obvious event.
- `video50`: the phase changes after the scissors cut and leave.
- `video64`: both structures are cut, the scissors leave, there is a short observation beat, and then the phase changes. This seemed closest to a reproducible rule.
- `video68`: cutting is followed by one or two seconds of observation; the phase changes before hook appearance.
- `video79`: similar to the short-observation rule, but the source video raised de-identification concerns.

### Candidate rule from the review

After the final required cut, allow a brief check that clipping and cutting are complete, then transition before the next purposeful gallbladder-dissection action. Do not rely exclusively on either scissors exit or hook appearance.

Preferred examples noted during review: `video64`, `video68`, and `video37`.

## P4 — Gallbladder dissection

### Initial working idea

P4 separates the gallbladder from the liver bed. Its end is difficult because detachment may be followed by Packaging or Cleaning/coagulation, and there may be a long ambiguous pause.

The recurring insight was to wait for evidence of the actual next task:

- bag introduction or clear movement to obtain/use a bag suggests Packaging;
- an irrigator, suction device, coagulation instrument, or deliberate liver-bed cleaning suggests Cleaning/coagulation;
- merely completing the last cut may not identify what comes next.

### Case observations

- `video01`: the transition clip does not show the separating cut; the phase changes while the camera is leaving the body. This is difficult to interpret visually.
- `video04`: after the final attachment is divided, there is a short check and then the phase changes.
- `video14`: visible irrigation/cleaning occurs while the source still labels P4; it later changes to Packaging as the camera leaves. The reviewer believed this should arguably contain a Cleaning phase before Packaging.
- `video19`: after detachment there is a wait while the next step is determined. The reviewer could partly see staff retrieving the bag because the footage was not fully de-identified. This supported waiting for evidence of the next task.
- `video37`: cleaning/coagulation begins with the hook, then a bipolar instrument replaces it; the supplied boundary falls between these changes. The important cue is that the next task has become cleaning/coagulation.
- `video44`: cutting ends, there is a wait, and the camera leaves, apparently to obtain the bag. Confirmation that a bag was actually retrieved remains useful.
- `video50`: the camera leaves and returns with a bag; the next task is Packaging.
- `video64`: the supplied transition occurs well before the irrigator appears, which is hard to reproduce visually.
- `video68`: cutting completed much earlier; the gallbladder is held during a long wait, the image briefly shows the doctor's foot, and the phase changes around that interruption.
- `video79`: the next task is Cleaning/coagulation, with Packaging later. The transition occurs a few seconds after cutting while the gallbladder is put down.

### Candidate rule from the review

Keep P4 until positive evidence identifies the next dominant task. Use purposeful bag activity for P5 or distinct cleaning/haemostasis activity for P6. Do not force one fixed successor.

Preferred examples noted during review: `video44` for movement towards Packaging and `video37` for movement towards Cleaning/coagulation.

## P5 — Gallbladder packaging

### Initial working idea

P5 covers placing and securing the detached gallbladder in a retrieval bag. Its start depends on the preceding task; its end is defined by what begins next.

If the next task is cleaning/coagulation, look for purposeful attention to the liver bed, irrigation, suction, or haemostasis. If the next task is extraction, look for the bag handle or packaged specimen being grasped and pulled. A brief touch without purposeful continuation should not trigger the boundary.

### Case observations

- `video01`: packaging completes, the bag is left, and inspection/cleaning begins.
- `video04`: after packaging, a grasper immediately starts manipulating the liver and the phase changes.
- `video14`: packaging is followed by pulling the specimen out.
- `video19`: after packaging, the camera focuses on the liver and the phase changes before the irrigator begins interacting. The precise source rule remains unclear.
- `video37`: extraction begins immediately even though the bag does not appear fully tied; the change follows the actual task rather than perfect bag closure.
- `video44`: a grasper touches the liver and cleaning/coagulation begins.
- `video50`: the phase changes when the bag's extraction handle is held.
- `video64`: likewise, the transition is associated with holding the extraction handle.

### Candidate rule from the review

End Packaging when another purposeful task begins: distinct liver-bed cleaning/haemostasis for P6, or sustained pulling of the packaged specimen for P7.

Preferred examples noted during review: `video01`, `video14`, and `video37`.

## P6 — Cleaning and coagulation

### Initial working idea

P6 includes irrigation, suction, liver-bed inspection, cleaning, and haemostatic coagulation when these become a distinct task. It is not fixed after Packaging: it may occur before Packaging, after Packaging, after an extraction attempt, more than once, or not at all.

### Case observations

- `video01`: irrigation/cleaning occurs; the supplied transition follows when this activity is left behind. The reviewer would have preferred the later visible grasp of the bag handle as the P7 cue.
- `video04`: the camera leaves the scene around the supplied transition to Retraction, making the exact cue unclear.
- `video14`: Retraction occurs first and Cleaning/coagulation is last. The transition to P6 happens when a grasper is reintroduced, although purposeful liver contact begins roughly ten seconds later.
- `video19`: during an apparent P6-to-P7 transition the bag handle is grasped, but the surgeon returns to viewing/coagulation before eventually pulling. This shows why a brief grasp alone is insufficient.
- `video79`, `video64`, and `video37`: Cleaning/coagulation occurs before Packaging.
- `video50`: Cleaning/coagulation is absent.

### Candidate rule from the review

Start P6 when cleaning, inspection, irrigation, suction, or haemostasis becomes the dominant purposeful task. Brief coagulation supporting active dissection should remain within that dissection phase. End P6 only when another task genuinely takes over. For extraction, require sustained withdrawal rather than a momentary bag-handle grasp.

## P7 — Gallbladder retraction / extraction

### Initial working idea

The Cholec80 name “Gallbladder retraction” is potentially confusing. The class describes retrieval or extraction of the packaged gallbladder, not ordinary traction on the gallbladder or other tissue during dissection.

The reviewer's shorthand was: choose the boundary when the bag handle is grasped. The P6 counterexample in `video19` refined this to require purposeful, sustained pulling rather than a brief grasp followed by more cleaning.

### Candidate rule from the review

Start P7 when the bag handle, bag neck, or packaged specimen is purposefully engaged and sustained withdrawal begins. End it when extraction finishes, the video ends during extraction, or another distinct task such as Cleaning/coagulation begins.

Preferred examples noted during review: `video14` and `video50`. `video14` also demonstrates that Retraction need not be the final phase because it is followed by Cleaning/coagulation.

## Unresolved questions retained from the review

- Does Packaging begin at first bag introduction, or only when placement of the specimen begins?
- How long or distinct must haemostasis be before it becomes P6 rather than part of P2 or P4?
- When packaging equipment appears before the final gallbladder attachment is divided, should P4 continue until detachment is complete?
- Which local instruments provide equivalent task cues when they differ from Cholec80 examples?

These questions are preserved for clinical review. They should not erase the concrete observations above.
